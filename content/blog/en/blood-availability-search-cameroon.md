When a mother bleeds after childbirth, a child has severe malaria-related anaemia, or a road accident victim arrives at the emergency room, blood is needed quickly. In Cameroon, families are still often asked to find it themselves — calling relatives, looking for donors, or going from hospital to hospital.

This article explains why blood is hard to find, how the OpesCare **Blood Finder** works, what its results do and do not mean, and how hospitals and blood banks can make their inventory visible.

## Why blood is hard to find

### Supply falls short of need

VOA reported in 2021 that the number of blood units collected in Cameroon fell sharply in 2020 compared with 2019, a drop national blood officials attributed largely to the COVID-19 pandemic. The same report noted that national estimates of annual need were far higher than what was collected.

The World Health Organization notes, more broadly, that blood donation rates in low-income countries remain much lower than in high-income countries, and encourages voluntary, unpaid donation as the safest and most sustainable source.

### Information is scattered

Even when a compatible unit exists somewhere in the city, the family at the bedside rarely knows where. Each hospital and blood bank knows its own fridge; nobody sees the whole picture.

### Time matters

For a haemorrhage, minutes count. Every phone call that ends with "we don't have it" is time lost.

## What the Blood Finder does

### One search, connected facilities

The [Blood Finder](https://opescare.com/network/blood-finder) lets anyone search for blood across hospitals and blood banks connected to OpesCare. You choose:

- **the blood group** — A, B, AB or O, and Rhesus positive or negative;
- **the component** — for example red cells, plasma or platelets;
- **the area** — by location or town.

For each facility that reports a match, you see the facility, its distance, contact details where provided, a map with in-app directions, and **when the inventory was last updated**.

### Only real, published inventory

The Blood Finder shows only what facilities themselves have published from their live inventory. OpesCare never fills the page with sample data, estimates or demo figures. If no connected facility reports the group and component you need, the page tells you so. That is frustrating — but it is the truth, and it stops you travelling to a hospital on the strength of a number nobody entered.

### Information, not a reservation

A Blood Finder result is information to act on. It is **not** a reservation and not a guarantee the unit is still available when you arrive. Always call the facility first, and let the treating team coordinate.

## What it does not replace

Blood transfusion is a clinical act, and OpesCare does not change who decides:

- The **treating clinician** decides whether a transfusion is needed and which component.
- The **blood bank** performs compatibility testing (cross-matching) and issues the unit.
- The **facility's own procedures** for requesting, transporting and administering blood still apply.

The Blood Finder shortens the search. It does not shorten safety checks.

## Step by step: using the Blood Finder

### 1. Get the exact request from the clinical team

Ask the doctor or nurse for the patient's blood group and Rhesus factor, the component needed, and the number of units. If you have a Health ID, your blood group may already be on your emergency profile.

### 2. Search

Open [opescare.com/network/blood-finder](https://opescare.com/network/blood-finder), select the group and component, and allow location or choose a town.

### 3. Compare results

Look at distance and the last-updated time together. A recent update is more reliable.

### 4. Call before you go

Call the blood bank or hospital, explain the request and follow their instructions. Many will need a formal request from the treating facility.

### 5. Think about replacement

Some facilities ask families to bring donors to replace units used. Ask what applies.

## Understanding what you are searching for

### Blood groups in plain language

Every person has a blood group in the ABO system — A, B, AB or O — and a Rhesus factor, positive or negative. Together they give the eight familiar groups, such as O positive or A negative. Compatibility matters: giving the wrong group can cause a serious reaction. That is why the laboratory always tests compatibility before a transfusion, even when the group is already known.

When you search the Blood Finder, select the exact group the clinical team gave you. Do not try to work out "compatible" alternatives yourself; that decision belongs to the blood bank and the doctor.

### Components in plain language

A single donation is often separated into components, each used for different needs:

- **Red cells** carry oxygen and are commonly used for severe anaemia or blood loss.
- **Plasma** contains clotting factors and is used in some bleeding problems.
- **Platelets** help blood clot and are used in particular conditions where platelet counts are very low.

Not every facility separates or stores every component. If a search for one component gives no result, ask the clinical team whether another option is acceptable — do not change the request yourself.

## Questions families often ask

**"The finder shows a unit. Can I just go and collect it?"** No. Blood is issued by the blood bank to the treating facility under its procedures, usually after a formal request and compatibility testing. Call first and follow their instructions.

**"Why does the hospital ask us to bring donors?"** Some facilities rely on family replacement donation to rebuild stock after a unit is used. Policies differ; ask the facility what applies.

**"The finder shows nothing near us. What now?"** Widen the area, check again later (inventory is updated as it changes), and ask the treating team whether they can contact other facilities directly. An empty result means no connected facility has reported that group and component — not that none exists anywhere.

**"Is my blood group on my Health ID enough?"** It helps the team start the search sooner, but the laboratory will still test before any transfusion.

## Why honest data matters more than full pages

It would be easy to make the Blood Finder look busier by showing estimated or sample inventory. OpesCare will not do that. In a blood emergency, a false "available" sends a family across the city for nothing and wastes the time the patient does not have. Every result on the Blood Finder comes from a facility's own live inventory, with the time it was last updated, so you can judge how fresh it is.

## The most important step: donate

No search tool creates blood. Supply depends on people who donate voluntarily and regularly. If you are healthy and eligible, ask your nearest hospital or blood bank how to donate — and encourage family and colleagues to do the same. Facilities that publish on the Blood Finder can also show where donations are most needed by keeping inventory honest.
